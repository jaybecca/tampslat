Project Brief vs Project Plan: What to Include

Start with the outcome

Before choosing a format or tool, define the result this work needs to achieve. Identify who will use the result, what decision or action it should support, and which information is essential. A clear outcome prevents unnecessary sections and keeps the work focused on the reader rather than the process.

A practical workflow

  1. Gather accurate source information and confirm what is known, assumed or still needs checking.
  2. Choose a simple structure that matches the audience and the task. Use clear headings and plain language.
  3. Prepare a first version, then review it against the original objective, relevant requirements and any agreed constraints.
  4. Ask the intended user or a colleague to check clarity. Correct missing details before sharing or publishing.
  5. Save a clean master copy, assign an owner and set a review date so the work remains useful.

Common mistakes to avoid

Do not rely on vague statements, copied boilerplate or unsupported claims. Keep dates, figures, names and links accurate. Avoid collecting personal or confidential information that is not needed. Where a task involves regulated, legal, tax, financial, employment or security requirements, check the rules that apply to your location and obtain qualified advice when appropriate.

Practical takeaway

Make repeat work easier

Reusable templates help teams work consistently without forcing every project into the same wording. Keep the structure stable, but adapt examples, details and instructions to the real situation. Review templates when customer needs, tools or requirements change.
