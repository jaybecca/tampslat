How to write a practical business plan

TAMPslat Journal · Practical guide

Start with the customer problem. Describe who experiences it, how they solve it today and why your offer is a better fit. Use evidence from interviews, enquiries, sales or credible market research rather than unsupported market-size claims.

Start with the real need

Explain the offer in plain language. List the product or service, delivery method, pricing logic, key suppliers and the capabilities required to deliver consistently. Be specific about what is included and excluded.

Build a practical approach

Build a simple financial view. Estimate sales volume, average transaction value, direct costs, overheads and cash timing. Show assumptions separately and test a cautious scenario as well as your expected case.

Put it into action

Finish with milestones for the next 90 days. Assign an owner, deadline and measure to each action. Review the plan when customer evidence, costs or operating conditions change.

Make the next step manageable
